The Stipulated Judgment

$26 Million, entered by stipulation.

Some numbers need precise labels, and this one gets it: a stipulated judgment, not a verdict, and not part of the firm's recovered totals.

The judgment, entered by stipulation for the full $26,000,000.00 against the individual defendant, said otherwise. Ruano de Diaz, Los Angeles Superior Court, 2026.

Results FAQs.

Do these results mean my case is worth millions?

No, and no honest lawyer would tell you otherwise. Every result on this page was earned on its own facts: its own liability picture, its own medicine, and its own available coverage. Prior results do not guarantee a similar outcome. What the results show is how this firm builds a case when the stakes are high.

What is the difference between a verdict, a settlement, and a judgment?

A verdict is what a jury awards after trial. A settlement is an agreement that resolves the case before a verdict, and it is how most cases end. A judgment is the court's final order in a case. A stipulated judgment is a judgment the court enters on terms the parties have agreed to.

What is a stipulated judgment?

A judgment entered by the court based on the parties' agreement rather than a jury's verdict. The $26 Million result on this page is a stipulated judgment entered against an individual defendant in Los Angeles Superior Court in 2026. We label it precisely because precision matters: it is not a verdict, and it is not counted in the firm's recovered totals.

What are TopVerdict rankings?

TopVerdict publishes annual rankings of the largest reported verdicts and settlements in the United States and in each state. Every ranking cited on this page was published by TopVerdict for the firm's actual results and is quoted exactly as issued, including the No. 1 California verdict of 2023 in its category.

Why are some results listed without names or details?

Because some resolutions are confidential, and some clients, particularly survivors of sexual assault, deserve their privacy. We list the amount and the nature of the matter, and nothing that could identify the client. A firm that trades client privacy for marketing is telling you how it will treat yours.

What does the $60 Million+ figure include?

Verdicts and settlements recovered for the firm's clients. It does not include the $26 Million stipulated judgment, which is reported separately on this page and is never folded into recovered totals. When we publish a number, the label on it is exact.

What actually drives results like these?

Preparation the other side can see. Liability proven with evidence rather than adjectives, medicine documented by treating physicians, future care priced through life-care planning, and a firm the defense knows will pick a jury. Carriers and defense counsel price cases on trial risk. These results are what that pricing looks like when the case is built.
