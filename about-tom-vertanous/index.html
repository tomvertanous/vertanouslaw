The Story

First generation.

Tom Vertanous is the founder of The Vertanous Firm. He is a first-generation Armenian-American who immigrated to the United States from Iran at the age of four. He was raised in Los Angeles, and he built his practice in the same city that raised him.

That history is not biography for its own sake. It is the shape of the practice. Tom serves clients in English, Armenian, and Farsi, because nobody should have to describe the worst season of their life through a translator. And he answers his own phone, because the person trusting you with their case deserves the lawyer, not a call center.

The firm's cases reflect the same conviction: catastrophic injury, public entity liability, traumatic brain injury, and wrongful death. The cases where the other side has resources, lawyers, and time, and the client has one firm. Start with the Pasadena personal injury overview, or see the results the approach has produced.

Education and Training

USC, Loyola, and a federal courtroom.

Tom earned his B.S. from the University of Southern California, studying Political Science and Health Care Administration, and his J.D. from Loyola Law School Los Angeles in 2019.

During law school, he was selected as one of two extern students for the Hon. Stephen V. Wilson of the U.S. District Court for the Central District of California. A federal externship is an education you cannot buy: how a judge actually reads a brief, what persuades, what irritates, and what wins. Tom carried that education into a plaintiff's practice, where every filing is written for the person who will rule on it.

Before the externship came internships that still shape the firm's work: the Cedars-Sinai Heart Institute, the Food and Drug Administration, and the Los Angeles County District Attorney's Sexual Crimes Division. The first two built the medical fluency that anchors the firm's injury practice. The third taught him how to work with survivors, and it is part of why the firm handles civil cases for survivors of sexual assault with the care they require.

The Medicine

A published medical author.

Most injury lawyers learn medicine from deposition transcripts. Tom published it. He is the first author of a peer-reviewed paper on increasing organ donation through the professionalization of organ procurement organizations, published in Transplantation Proceedings in 2016: Vertanous T, et al., Leading Efforts to Increase Organ Donation Through Professionalization of Organ Procurement, Transplant Proc 2016;48(1):10-14, PMID 26915835.

Why does that matter to a client with a herniated disc or a brain injury? Because injury cases are medical cases. The lawyer who understands the imaging, the diagnosis, and the treatment plan builds a different case than the lawyer who forwards records to an expert and hopes. Tom reads the records himself, works with treating physicians as a colleague rather than a customer, and prices future care through life-care planning before the first demand goes out.

Nowhere does that matter more than in traumatic brain injury cases, where the defense strategy is almost always to shrink the medicine: to call a concussion minor, to call the symptoms subjective, to call the client recovered. A firm fluent in the medicine does not let that stand.

The Record

Built for trial, proven at trial.

Credentials open doors. Verdicts close arguments. The record extends beyond one trial.
